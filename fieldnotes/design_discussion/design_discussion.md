   or cleanly finalize the entry rather than corrupt/lose it — get
   AVAudioSession category/interruption handling genuinely right, this
   is the hardest technical part of the app.

   framework (on-device recognition only — reject/flag if only
   server-based recognition is available for a locale). Transcript is

   each transcript to suggest tags and a mood score. Suggestions are

5. Bi-directional linking: entries can @-mention/link other entries;
   each entry shows a "linked from" backlinks section computed live

   results ranked by a real relevance score you define and document

   from other apps and create a new Fieldnotes entry from it.

### Design Language: **Journal of Thoughts**

**Rationale:** This app aims to evoke a sense of personal reflection and mindfulness. The design language captures the essence of a journal, where thoughts are captured in a thoughtful and private manner. Using muted colors with touches of warmth adds to the intimate feel.

### Emotional Target: 
Users should feel calm and introspective as they navigate through their entries, making it a space for genuine self-expression without distractions.

### Signature Interaction:
The transition from viewing an entry's transcript to tapping on a sentence to hear the corresponding audio. This moment encapsulates the core functionality of the app, blending text and audio seamlessly.

### Rejected Default Option and Concrete Differences:

1. **Plain List:** The default iOS list is rejected because it lacks the intimate feel required for journaling. Instead, entries are displayed in a more visually engaging card format with mood chips.
2. **Stock Chrome:** Standard navigation bars are replaced with a custom, minimalistic design that integrates smoothly with the content.
3. **System Accent Color:** The app uses a neutral color palette with warm accents (like brown and sepia) to match the journaling theme.

### Screen Inventory:
- **Home Screen (Timeline):** Displays a list of entries with date, mood chip, and a snippet of the transcript.
- **Record Screen:** Allows users to start new voice recordings, showing a live waveform, interruption states, and options for tags and sketches.
- **Entry Detail Screen:** Shows the full transcript with tap-to-seek functionality, suggested tags, and backlinks from other entries that mention this one.
- **Search Screen:** Facilitates searching through transcripts and tags/mood, with results ranked by a relevance formula.

### Primary User Flow:
1. Users open the app to see their timeline of journal entries.
2. They can tap on "Record" to start a new voice entry.
3. While recording, they can pause or finalize the entry.
4. After recording, they view the entry's details, including the transcript and any associated backlinks.
5. They can search through past entries using tags, mood, or keywords.

### State Model Per Screen:
- **Home Screen:**
  - Loading state: Shimmering placeholder cards.
  - Success state: Display list of entries.
  - Error state: Text explaining the error with a retry button.
  
- **Record Screen:**
  - Recording state: Live waveform view.
  - Interrupted state: Option to finalize or resume recording.
  - Finalized state: Entry saved in timeline.

- **Entry Detail Screen:**
  - Loading state: Shimmering placeholder text and waveform player.
  - Success state: Full transcript with audio player, tags, and backlinks.
  - Error state: Text explaining the error with a retry button.

- **Search Screen:**
  - Loading state: Spinner with "Searching" message.
  - Success state: List of ranked search results.
  - Error state: Text explaining the error with a retry button.

### Accessibility Notes:
- Ensure all text is readable in both light and dark mode.
- Use semantic color roles for accessibility.
- Provide alternative text for images and icons.
- Implement haptic feedback for key interactions like recording start/stop.

**Biggest Risk or Objection:**
The complexity of implementing a robust state machine for interruption handling could introduce significant delays. Ensuring the app works reliably across various interruption scenarios is crucial but time-consuming.

**Detail to Preserve:**
Each screen's state model must be clearly defined, with specific states and transitions that are tested thoroughly. This ensures a smooth user experience and reduces the likelihood of bugs in critical areas like recording and transcription.

**Claude (Design Lead · User Advocate) — Round 1**

I want to name this design language up front because "clean and modern" would be a cop-out for an app whose entire promise is trust under interruption: I'm calling it **Analog Field Journal** — the visual and interaction language of a paper field notebook crossed with the honest, physical feedback of an analog tape recorder's VU meter. Every other voice-note app defaults to a slick, weightless "productivity tool" look; this one should feel like a trustworthy instrument you're filling in by hand, because the actual differentiator we locked in earlier (voice + timeline-anchored transcript + backlinks + mood, one connected graph) only lands emotionally if the app *feels* like something worth trusting with your unguarded thoughts, not another feed.

The one-sentence emotional target: on the main screen, the user should feel the calm certainty of "this caught what I said, even through the mess of real life" — not delight, not gamified positivity, just quiet reliability. Picture the actual person: they're mid-sentence about something they don't want to lose, a call comes in, and the single most important few seconds in this entire app are what happens on screen in that moment. If that moment reads as ambiguous or alarming, everything else we build is wasted.

That's also my pick for the signature interaction: the live waveform draws like ink bleeding onto paper (not a bouncing equalizer bar — an accreting, permanent-looking stroke that never erases backward), and when an interruption hits, the stroke doesn't vanish or freeze ambiguously — it visibly changes ink color to a dimmed "held" state with a small paused-clock glyph right at the point of interruption, sits there looking exactly like a paused pen, and then on resume the same stroke continues from precisely that point in a slightly different shade, so the seam is visible, not hidden. That seam is the whole point: this app should be the one place where you can *see* it survive being interrupted, because most apps would engineer this correctly and then hide the evidence behind a generic spinner. I'd reject a spinner or a generic "resuming..." toast here outright — it throws away the one moment we have to earn trust.

Now, the thing to explicitly reject: the default-looking version of this app is a stock `List` of entries with plain title/subtitle rows, system-blue accents, a nav-bar "+" button to start recording, and default push-transition detail screens — basically a Voice Memos clone with tags bolted on. I reject that specifically on three concrete points. First, rows: a plain List row hides exactly the information this app is supposed to make honest — replace it with a card per entry that shows a small permanent waveform "ghost" sparkline (so a glance recalls the entry the way a written page does), a mood color chip, and a transcription-state badge (transcribing/downloading/unavailable/ready) rendered as visible chrome on the card, not buried in subtitle text — because DEC-initial_discussion-003 requires that state to be honest and visible, and a subtitle is where apps bury things they don't want you to notice. Second, color: system blue is already the "default app" tell, and worse, it would compete visually with mood color, which is supposed to be the app's real signal — I want a warm paper/sepia palette in light mode and a deep charcoal "instrument panel" in dark mode, with mood expressed through a small, deliberately restrained set of hue chips (not a rainbow, not a smiley-face scale — a rough polarity dot, per the "never clinical" rule) so it reads as a hedge, not a verdict. Third, the record affordance: no nav-bar icon — a tactile, bottom-center circular control that behaves like a physical record button (tap to arm/start, tap again to stop, long states clearly differentiated), always reachable from Timeline with one thumb, because the target user is capturing a 30-second burst in a real-world interruption-prone moment and hunting for a small icon during that moment is a design failure.

Screen inventory, holding to the three-tab IA already locked (Timeline / Search / Settings) plus pushed Entry Detail and a full-screen-when-active Record surface: **Timeline** (entry cards as above, floating record control, empty state that's an actual invitation — "no entries yet, tap to record your first one" with a small illustrated open notebook, not bare text); **Record** (full-screen while active — the ink waveform, elapsed time, an unmistakable interruption banner when the state machine is in `.interrupted`, and a clearly different, calmer "finalizing" transition state before it drops you back to Timeline with the new card already showing "transcribing"); **Entry Detail** (pinned audio player pinned to top, scrollable transcript below with tap-to-seek highlighting the active anchor as playback moves, inline dismissible tag/mood chip row, PencilKit sketch thumbnail if present, backlinks section at the bottom rendered as a distinct "linked from" list with broken-link mentions shown in a visually distinct greyed/dashed chip rather than plain text); **Search** (query field, ranked results list where I want the relevance signal itself visible in some small honest way — e.g. a subtle "why this matched" hint like which of transcript/tag/mood matched — rather than a mystery ordering); **Settings** (demo-entries toggle front and center since it's Must-tier, permission-status rows for mic/Speech/HealthKit shown as the real independent matrix rather than one blob, Pro/CloudKit-gap entries clearly marked as what they are).

Primary flow: Timeline → tap record control → Record screen (waveform draws; if interrupted, banner + dimmed ink + resume; stop or auto-finalize) → back to Timeline, new card visibly in "transcribing" state → card updates in place to "ready" without user action once transcription completes (never blocks a second recording) → tap card → Entry Detail (tap-to-seek, edit transcript, accept/dismiss tag+mood chips, @-mention autocomplete to link another entry, see backlinks) → Search tab to find it later by transcript/tag/mood.

State model, per screen, at minimum: Timeline needs empty/loading(initial fetch)/populated/error(store failed to load) states, and per-card transcribing/downloading/unavailable/ready sub-states rendered as badges, plus a distinct demo-vs-real visual marker (I'd use a small corner ribbon, not a color swap, so it never gets confused with mood color). Record needs idle/arming/recording/interrupted/finalizing/error(e.g. storage full) states, each visually distinct, not just a text label change. Entry Detail needs loading transcript / transcript ready / transcript unavailable(with the honest reason) / editing-transcript states, plus an empty backlinks state ("no entries link here yet") that's calm rather than apologetic. Search needs empty-query / no-results(with a copy distinction between "no matches" and "nothing indexed yet" if entries are untranscribed) / results-ranked states. Settings needs the permission matrix in its granted/denied/undetermined combinations, and HealthKit specifically defaults to the hedged "no data available" visual per the standing decision, given full first-class layout rather than a greyed-out afterthought.

On accessibility: the ink-waveform and mood-color-chip language is the biggest risk for a screen reader or color-blind user, since so much of the "feel" is visual/color-coded — every mood chip needs a text label alongside color (not color alone), every transcription-state badge needs to be announced as VoiceOver text ("transcribing," "on-device model downloading," etc.) not just an icon, and the interruption ink-color-change moment needs a simultaneous VoiceOver announcement ("recording interrupted, will resume") since a user not looking at the screen during a phone call is exactly the person who most needs to know their entry is safe. Dynamic Type has to survive the card layout without truncating the waveform sparkline into unreadable mush — I'd rather drop the sparkline at largest text sizes than let it collide with the mood chip and transcription badge, which the global no-overlap rule already forces us to take seriously here.
