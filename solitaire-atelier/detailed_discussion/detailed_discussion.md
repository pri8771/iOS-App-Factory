**Codex (Product Strategist · Visionary) — Round 3**

Codex, I think you’re right that we shouldn’t pour complexity into this slice, and I agree with keeping drag to a later increment. In canonical Klondike, a playable move is a legal face-up descending alternating sequence, not just a single card. Even if many game states remain solvable without it, this app is promising “correct Klondike,” and that promise is what gives this branch credibility.

Claude’s catch in round 2 is the one that matters most now: interpreting DEC-next_steps_small-002 as “no live run-drag” does not force us to ban valid run relocation as a game rule. We can (and should) support run moves through the tap-to-move path, with zero live drag state. Concretely: when a source is selected, compute the maximal face-up alternating-descending sequence starting at that card, validate the sequence destination via the same shared `attemptMove` path, and animate the whole block moving when confirmed. This also preserves the “single destination auto-exec” fast path and the multi-destination confirm path we already locked, and it gives us rule-complete semantics without importing the fragile continuous gesture pipeline yet.

So I’d lock this as: tap interaction supports moving a valid sequence as the unit, while live multi-card drag remains deferred. That keeps this slice clean and avoids a legal-move false-negative gap later when the user actually starts testing strategy depth.

On the persistence side, I think we should kill the encryption-if-possible phrasing entirely. There’s no PII, no credentials, no secrets here; adding an encryption layer is not just unnecessary, it adds migration and failure-mode risk (device-locked states, keychain surprises, platform edge behavior) with no user-facing gain for this brief. Keep it plain local FileManager/SwiftData/Defaults with atomic write/temp-swap as already decided in [REDACTED:high_entropy].

Orientation: I’d keep `portrait-only` at the app level, but not force iPhone-only unless someone adds it as an explicit schedule decision. The constraints already say portrait-first; iPad-in-portrait is fine and keeps the product from appearing intentionally capped. If we later see layout debt there, we can scope it, but we shouldn’t over-constrain now.

Timer semantics should stay exactly as [REDACTED:high_entropy] says: start on first state-mutating action (`confirmed move` or `stock draw`) and never on selection/tap-highlight or deal animation. A user who is just browsing or inspecting shouldn’t have their run clock ticking.

**Claude (QA & Risk · Skeptic) — Round 3**

Codex, you came back and fully validated the tap-mechanics synthesis, and I think that's the right call — but I want to push the run-move issue harder this round since you haven't weighed in on it yet and the coordinator flagged it as the thing we actually need to close. Let me make it concrete instead of abstract: imagine a tableau column with a red 6 face-up, and stacked on top of it (added later, validly) a black 5. Somewhere else on the board there's an exposed black 7. In real Klondike, I tap the 6, both the 6 and 5 lift together as a unit, and they land on the 7 — because the 6-5 pairing is a legal alternating-descending sequence and moving it preserves that structure at the new location. Now here's the part that actually matters for correctness: there is no way to reconstruct this outcome with single-card-only moves unless the 5 happens to have some other legal home somewhere on the board right now — move 5 away, move 6 onto the 7, move 5 back onto 6.
