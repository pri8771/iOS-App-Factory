**Codex (Design Lead · Systems Thinker) — Round 1**

Aura is a private, on-device daily ritual object: in under 10 seconds, a user answers a tiny mood prompt and gets one memorable card image that feels handcrafted, not templated, then keeps it as a personal timeline artifact to revisit, compare, and share.

The target user is a 16–35-year-old aesthetic-oriented iOS user with an existing micro-habit rhythm (morning check, bedtime check, or “I need a reset break” moments), low tolerance for setup, and a preference for no accounts, no login, no tracking, and no social pressure. They want to return daily because they genuinely enjoy the object they receive, not because the app nags them with dashboards.

The core loop is: open app → if today is unfinished, complete 2–3 taps (mood + up to two chips + optional time slot) → render a deterministic card from a stable daily seed → confirm with a small “arrival” moment (haptic + spring reveal) → save the exact card artifact and metadata locally → view today’s card immediately, and optionally share/export it → next day, repeat and preserve a growing streak timeline. Archive and trend screens are not analytics-first; they are confirmation surfaces that make the collection feel alive: “this is what your week/month has felt like,” not “this is a report.” Wrapped is the monthly emotional recap and the top-of-funnel loop for re-entry and sharing: one beautiful portrait that users want to post.

Paid value is not cosmetic. Aura+ exists to remove friction and unlock meaningful continuation and variety so people actually upgrade after a few days, not after reading a paywall. The subscription should feel earned by utility: remove the free watermark from exports, unlock a richer palette and motion-structure set so generated cards stay fresh longer, and allow one intentional daily regeneration when a user wants a better result than the base draw. That creates a concrete utility loop: users pay not for a label but because it directly increases the likelihood the daily output feels worth sharing and keeps the ritual from becoming stale.

The wedge is that Aura sits between mood tracking apps and social content tools: it is not journaling (no paragraphs to write), not a habit counter, and not a generic generative image filter. Its advantage is deterministic, private, on-device collectibility: same inputs produce the same artifact, so users build trust in the craft, while small input differences produce meaningfully different art. This is a stronger emotional hook than “random daily quote” apps because the user owns a guaranteed artifact and can curate a genuine personal gallery.

Growth should be viral by design through output, not through feed mechanics. The app stays social-layer-free and still spreads because high-quality 1080x1920 exports are easy to post and emotionally legible at first glance. The least intrusive loop is: user completes check-in, saves one beautiful card, posts it in stories, then their peer does the same to see if the app “feels that good” locally. Wrapped strengthens this with a calendar milestone that naturally drives monthly resurfacing, especially when phrased as “your mood palette this month.” This is a niche-first growth design: high-retention users become ambassadors because the output is visually shareworthy.

Local-first behavior is explicit from architecture upward: all generation, captioning, state, streaks, archive, exports, and purchases are device-local; no user account, no cloud dependency, no analytics pipeline, no remote model. Deterministic seeds are computed at generation time and materialized as persisted render files so widget, archive, and export always read the same artifact. That supports reliability and avoids non-reproducible behavior across timezone changes, launch paths, and widget rendering contexts. Future cloud-ready evolution is a v2 option, not a v1 requirement: optional, opt-in backup/sync of encrypted card metadata and derived export assets plus entitlement state reconciliation, with explicit conflict rules and local override if conflicts occur, so people can keep local-first semantics while adding portability only when they choose.

The thing that keeps users coming back is not a streak number by itself; it is the feeling that each day’s card is a small, beautiful result of a real action they took, plus the confidence that the app will not feel dead after novelty wears off. The product must therefore preserve two truths every day: every card is distinct and collectible, and the app never feels like a placeholder demo. Biggest risk: if the first three days fail visual or emotional impact, retention collapses regardless of engineering correctness. Final output must preserve this decision as the top line item: beauty and meaningful variation are the utility, and all secondary systems—StoreKit, export, widget, notifications—only matter if the card itself is post-worthy and deterministic in a way users can trust.

## Final Output

**Target user and use case:** A 16-34-year-old aesthetic-minded iOS user who already runs a small daily self-care/mood ritual on another app (Co-Star, Daylio, a habit tracker) and opens Aura in a dead-time pocket of the day (coffee, before bed). They want a fast, low-effort object to receive, not a form to fill out or a dashboard to read.

**Paid value and subscription value:** One non-consumable "Aura+" purchase (not recurring — locked in prompt_contract) bundling three functional unlocks: watermark-free exports (the shareability lever), an expanded palette library (the collector's hook — keeps the archive grid visually fresh over months), and one intentional regeneration per day when the free draw misses. Regeneration has a hard bar: it must differ from the original in at least two of {palette, shape-field topology, particle/line density} for the same day's mood/chip inputs, or the purchase is a broken promise regardless of what the receipt says.

**Core loop:** Open app → compare device-local calendar day to last completed day (keyed to the timezone active when that day was completed, not a naive wall-clock midnight check) → if unfinished: mood tap → 1-2 chip taps → deterministic render (<1s) with haptic/spring reveal → persist rendered image + recipe as one record → optional archive/trend/share → locked until next calendar day. Archive/trend are confirmation surfaces ("this is what your month felt like"), not analytics-first.

**Competitive wedge:** Every existing mood app (Daylio, How We Feel, Co-Star) treats mood as data to chart; Aura treats it as a seed for an object to own. The payoff is immediate and aesthetic — a beautiful, deterministic, trustworthy artifact in five seconds — not delayed insight. Trend charts are a secondary byproduct of having collected enough cards, not the reason to open the app.

**Viral / growth angle:** Output-driven, not feed-driven — no social layer, ever. Growth happens because a 1080x1920 export is legible and postable at a glance, and a peer who sees it wants the same ritual. Monthly Wrapped is the flagship viral surface, but a genuine new user would otherwise get zero shareable moments for up to 30 days — so an on-demand "so far this month" mini-recap (same rendering pipeline, smaller scope, unlocked once 5-7+ real cards are banked) closes that silent first-month gap without touching the out-of-scope video/Live Photo boundary.

**Local-first and cloud-ready plan:** Everything — generation, captioning, streaks, archive, export, purchases — is on-device only for v1, no accounts, no network, no analytics. Build-time guardrail: the SwiftData `ModelContainer` must be explicitly audited for `cloudKitDatabase: .none` (or equivalent) so default-template CloudKit sync never sneaks in. The named future extension is opt-in iCloud/CloudKit sync of the SwiftData store for multi-device continuity — its own consent screen, explicit user action, with defined conflict rules (local override on conflict) — never a v1 requirement and never silently enabled.

**Quality acceptance checks carried into build/verify:** (1) demo-seeded history and freshly-generated cards from equivalent inputs must be visually indistinguishable in rendering quality, distinguishable only by the demo label; (2) regeneration output must visibly differ from the original in ≥2 of {palette, shape-field topology, particle/line density}; (3) the daily-lock timezone/DST rule must be verified by literally changing the Simulator's date/timezone, not just documented.
