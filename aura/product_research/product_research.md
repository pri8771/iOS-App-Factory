## Original Prompt

```
Build a visually stunning, fully offline iOS app called "Aura" — a
daily generative "vibe card." Native Apple stack only: SwiftUI +
SwiftData, Core Image / Metal (CIFilter compositions or a custom
Metal shader) for procedural generative art, Swift Charts for the
recap view, StoreKit 2 for local in-app purchases (no backend needed —
App Store handles receipts/entitlements locally). No accounts, no
network, no third-party SDKs — everything on-device, forever. Follow
the ios-app-factory-rules.

CONCEPT: once a day, the user answers 2-3 quick prompts (a mood
picker, one or two tap-to-select word/emoji chips, optionally the
on-device time of day). From that input plus a per-day deterministic
seed, generate a unique abstract generative-art "Aura" card — think
painterly gradient blooms, particle fields, or flow-field line art,
NOT a literal illustration — paired with a short generated caption
built from a curated local phrase bank (never a hallucinated/unsafe
LLM call — this is deterministic, on-device, and instant). The card
must look genuinely beautiful: this is the single most important
requirement in this build. If the generative output looks like
placeholder gradients or a design-101 exercise, the build has failed
its actual goal even if it compiles and passes gates.

CORE FEATURES (favor complete over broad):
1. Daily check-in flow: fast (under 10 seconds), delightful, satisfying
   micro-interactions (haptics, spring animations) on every tap. Once
   completed, locked until the next calendar day (device local time
   zone) — handle timezone travel and DST without allowing two cards
   the "same day" or skipping a day.
2. Generative card engine: a real, documented deterministic algorithm
   mapping (date seed + mood + chips) -> a reproducible but visually
   varied composition (palette, shape field, motion-blur/particle
   density). Same inputs on the same day always render the same card;
   different days must feel meaningfully different from each other,
   not like the same template recolored. This is the technical heart
   of the app — treat it with the same rigor as an algorithm module,
   not incidental UI.
3. Collection & streaks: a scrollable archive/grid of past cards
   (like a photo grid), a streak counter, and a "your Aura over time"
   trend view (mood distribution via Swift Charts).
4. Monthly/seasonal "Wrapped": after each calendar month, generate a
   shareable recap — a short animated sequence or a single composite
   poster of that month's dominant palette/mood story (Spotify-Wrapped
   style). This is a primary viral surface — must render as a
   beautiful, portrait (9:16) export.
5. Share export: every single card AND the monthly Wrapped must export
   as a high-resolution image (and ideally a short looping video/Live
   Photo-style clip of the generative animation) sized correctly for
   Instagram/TikTok Stories (1080x1920), via the native share sheet.
   A small, tasteful, dismissible-only-via-purchase watermark on free
   exports is the monetization hook (see below) — it must never be so
   intrusive it kills the shareability that drives virality.
6. Monetization (StoreKit 2, local only): one non-consumable "Aura+"
   unlock via native IAP removing the export watermark and unlocking
   extra palette themes + the ability to regenerate a card once per
   day if unsatisfied (free tier: no regeneration). Handle purchase,
   restore purchases, and the Simulator's inability to complete real
   StoreKit transactions honestly — use a local StoreKit configuration
   file for Simulator testing and say so explicitly, don't fake a
   purchase succeeding without going through StoreKit's real API.
7. Widget: home-screen widget showing today's card once generated, or
   an inviting "your Aura is waiting" state before check-in.
8. Notifications (local only, opt-in): a gentle daily reminder if the
   user hasn't checked in yet, respecting Focus/Do Not Disturb norms
   and permission denial gracefully.

PRIORITIES, in order: (a) the generative art must be genuinely
beautiful and varied — treat this as a design/craft problem, iterate
on the actual visual output, not just "does it render"; (b) the
deterministic seeding algorithm must be correct and tested (same
inputs -> same card, always); (c) the share-export pipeline must
produce real, correctly-sized, good-looking image/video files, not a
plain screenshot of the UI. Seed a "demo history" toggle with 30+ days
of varied past cards so the archive/Wrapped/trend screens are never
empty on first review. Provide a clear way to run it in the iOS
Simulator via Xcode, and honestly flag anything StoreKit-transaction-
related that cannot be fully verified without a real device/sandbox
account.
```

ASSUMPTION: Daylio-style mood trackers do routine logging and streaks well, but they are data-first and rarely deliver high-art generative visuals from the same deterministic ritual.  
ASSUMPTION: Reflectly and similar reflective journaling apps do onboarding and emotional tone well, but lean on writing/LLM-like narratives and usually require heavier content systems than this brief allows.  
ASSUMPTION: Spotify Wrapped and similar recap experiences excel at seasonal storytelling and bragging value, which is the right benchmark for Wrapped.  
ASSUMPTION: Wallpaper/art generator apps create visual novelty, but typically optimize for single-shot aesthetics and not daily determinism or lockstep state logic.  
Gap exploited by Aura is the combination of deterministic daily seed-based generative art + mood ritual + streak continuity + share-ready monthly recap, all on-device and network-free.

For platform expectations, iPhone-native behavior is the real differentiator: ASSUMPTION from product practice is that users judge an iOS utility less by feature count and more by interaction feel (tap micro-feedback, transitions, widget coherence, share frictionlessness). That means the card should feel like it belongs in the ecosystem: native haptics and spring interactions on every decision, a widget that cleanly reflects pending/completed state, and native share/export flows instead of custom copy UI. The biggest platform leverage point is not “new feature work,” it is native rhythm: daily time-based state (calendar/tz/DST-sensitive), local notification etiquette, and a first-screen feeling that checks in one pass.

A skeptical read of the brief shows risks that can quietly break perceived product quality if ignored. The highest-risk unknown is art quality: a deterministic engine can be technically correct but feel generic if the parameterization collapses into recolor-only variation. The second risk is temporal logic; DST + timezone + backward clock edits can break lock semantics and can instantly destroy trust. The third is monetization optics; a free watermark must be unobtrusive enough for social sharing yet valuable enough to justify upgrade, and StoreKit behavior in Simulator has hard limits that can’t be hand-waved. The fourth is demo history credibility; synthetic cards used to avoid empty states must never pollute real streak/counter semantics.

Implications for the first build: start from three immutable pieces—the check-in finite-state flow, deterministic seed model, and export pipeline—and make every other screen orbit them. On day one, implement a strict state machine (pending/today-complete/blocked-by-day), explicit loading/empty/success/error states, and a visible “Demo data” mode label so fake data is honest by design. Keep premium boundaries simple and testable: unlock only watermark removal + regen once-per-day + extra palettes. Preserve speed by making generation almost instant, with animation budget focused on transitions and feedback, not runtime-heavy re-composition. The first-version bar should be “daily ritual and deterministic beauty work every day” before adding any optional extras like video export.

**Claude (QA & Risk · Pragmatist) — Round 1**

Thinking about who actually opens an app like this every day: this is squarely a journaling-adjacent, aesthetic-driven audience — probably skewing 16-30, already comfortable with Co-Star, Daylio, How We Feel, Apple's own Journal and State of Mind features (both on-device, both iOS 17+, both explicitly avoiding cloud requirements — that's the platform norm we should match, not exceed). The closest direct pattern-match isn't a mood tracker, it's a "daily card pull" app like Co-Star's daily horoscope card or a tarot app like Sanctuary — quick tap-in, single generated card, share-to-story loop. Spotify Wrapped is the explicit reference for the monthly recap, and that's a good north star, but I'd flag all of this as ASSUMPTION since I don't have live research access this phase — it's pattern-matching from general knowledge, not a fetched source.

The elephant in the room from a risk standpoint has nothing to do with code quality: apps in this exact category (mood trackers, astrology-vibe apps, daily-ritual journaling) have a well-known shape — huge day-1 install curiosity, brutal day-7/day-30 dropoff, because the entire value prop depends on a habit forming before the novelty wears off. No amount of shader quality fixes that; it just means the widget and the notification are the actual retention mechanism, not the art. I'd treat the widget as more important to get right than the notification, since notification permission denial is common and, once denied, that channel is just gone — the widget sitting on the home screen is the only remaining daily nudge for a user who said no to push.

That leads to a concrete platform constraint I want on the record now, because it'll bite hard if discovered late: WidgetKit timelines run in a very constrained process with tight CPU/memory/time budgets. You cannot re-run a CIFilter chain or a custom Metal shader live inside a widget the way you would in the main app — that's a good way to get the widget silently failing to update or timing out. The architecture has to render the card once, at the moment check-in completes (or at demo-seed time), persist it as an actual rendered image (plus the recipe/parameters for later re-derivation if needed), and have both the widget and the share-export pipeline read that cached image rather than re-invoking the generative engine. This also resolves a subtler determinism trap: if "time of day" is part of the seed and the seed is recomputed live every time the archive or widget displays a past day, you'd get a different rendering depending on when you look at it. The seed must be resolved and frozen at the moment the card is generated, and everything downstream (archive thumbnails, widget, export) reads the frozen result, never recomputes.

Second real risk: Simulator-rendered Metal/Core Image output is not guaranteed to look identical to on-device output — color space handling, GPU family differences, and Metal shader compilation can differ between Simulator (running translated on Apple Silicon) and a physical device. If the visual-review checkpoint the group already agreed to is done purely in Simulator, that's a real gap worth naming honestly rather than pretending Simulator review is equivalent to on-device review.

Third: the Wrapped feature is the flagship viral surface but it's structurally invisible for most of a real user's first month — it only fires at a calendar-month boundary. Demo history covers first-review, that's fine, but for the real (non-demo) case I'd want an explicit "this month so far" partial-Wrapped preview available on demand, otherwise the single most important growth feature in the app sits dormant for weeks after every fresh install, which is a bad first-run story if anyone actually uses this beyond a demo.

Fourth, purely pragmatic: high-res export means rendering the composition at 1080x1920 through a CIContext, which is meaningfully more GPU/memory work than the on-screen preview resolution — that needs to happen off the main thread, and should be sanity-checked against memory pressure on older supported devices, not just tested on whatever simulator happens to be running.

On sequencing, the smallest defensible v1 is: nail the deterministic engine and one genuinely gorgeous card renderer first, wire it to the check-in flow and SwiftData persistence, seed the demo history so archive/streak/trend/Wrapped aren't empty, and get static image export working end to end. Widget, notifications, and Wrapped's animated variant are real but secondary — they depend on the core engine being solid and the cached-render architecture being in place, and video/Live Photo export should be treated as fully out of scope for v1 rather than attempted and abandoned, since that's exactly the kind of stretch work that eats the time budget for the thing that's actually graded (the card has to be beautiful, not merely exportable as a video).
