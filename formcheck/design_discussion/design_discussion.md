"Formcheck". Native Apple stack only: SwiftUI + SwiftData, Vision

account entitlement, record that as a gap rather than omitting or
faking the feature silently.

Core features (complete over broad — ship fewer things fully working):

1. Camera-based rep counting: record a set using the front or back
   camera; run Vision body-pose detection in real time (or near-real-
   time on-device, whichever is honestly achievable) to count reps for
   a small set of exercises (squat, push-up, plank hold) and flag at
   least one concrete form issue per exercise (e.g., squat depth,
   push-up elbow angle) — the flagging logic must be a real, documented
   geometric rule on the pose joints, not a placeholder.
2. Exercise classification: a small on-device Core ML model (or a
   documented, justified fallback heuristic if training/bundling a
   custom model is genuinely out of scope for this project — record
   that decision honestly either way) that classifies which exercise
   is being performed from the pose stream, so the user doesn't have
   to manually select it every set.
3. Session history: SwiftData-persisted workout sessions (exercise,
   rep count, duration, flagged form issues, optional recorded clip
   reference) with a calendar/list view and per-exercise trend charts
   (Swift Charts).
4. HealthKit write: on session completion, with explicit permission,
   write a HKWorkout entry (type, duration, and any derived energy
   estimate you can honestly compute on-device — do not invent
   biometric numbers you have no basis for). Must degrade gracefully,
   with the session still fully saved locally, if permission is denied.
5. Apple Watch companion (separate watchOS target): shows live rep
   count and elapsed time on the wrist during a set, mirrored from the
   iPhone via WatchConnectivity, plus a haptic tap on every counted
   rep. Handle the Watch being unreachable/unpaired/app not installed
   without crashing or hanging the iPhone side.
6. Mac Catalyst target: browse past sessions and step through a
   recorded clip frame-by-frame with the detected pose skeleton
   overlaid, for reviewing form after the fact on a bigger screen.
7. Settings: manage which exercises are enabled, HealthKit permission
   status (with a re-request path), and a full local data export/erase
   (no data leaves the device implicitly, ever).

Priorities, in order: (a) honest reporting of every Simulator/hardware/
entitlement limitation encountered — this is the primary thing being

**Claude (Design Lead · User Advocate) — Round 1**

I want to name this app's design language "the quiet spotter" — think of a competent training partner standing just behind your shoulder who watches closely and only speaks up when something's actually wrong, then goes quiet again. That's the whole emotional target in one line: on the main screen the user should feel watched-over and taken seriously, not gamified, not chatty, not judged. Everything about the visual language should reinforce "one honest voice, used sparingly" rather than a dashboard of metrics competing for attention.

The signature interaction, and the thing I'd protect hardest if scope gets squeezed, is the rep-completion moment itself: a large tabular-numeral counter that gives a physical-feeling pulse and scale-bounce paired with the haptic on every counted rep, and when a geometric rule actually fires, a single warm amber annotation traces itself along the specific joint/angle on screen for about two seconds — like a coach's finger drawing the line — with the exact wording of the rule ("hips rose above shoulder line before full extension"), then it settles into the running log. That's the one moment in the whole app where the "real geometric rule, not a placeholder" promise becomes visible and felt, not just true in code, and it has to read as deliberate and rare, not as a constant stream of toasts.

If I imagine the default, un-designed version of this app, it's a TabView with Home/History/Settings, a stock camera preview with a green checkmark when a body is detected, a small rep count label in a corner, a plain List of past sessions with system blue accents and default row chrome, and a Settings screen that buries "known limitations" as a line item under About. I'm rejecting that outright, and here's what's concretely different: first, the rep tally is not a small corner label, it's the dominant visual element of the in-session screen because reps are the entire point of the product — everything else is secondary to it. Second, amber is reserved exclusively for form-flag moments and never used decoratively elsewhere in the app (not in buttons, not in charts, not in nav) — so amber becomes a learned, trustworthy signal rather than a rotating brand color. Third, all counters and timers use a monospaced/tabular numeral style specifically to feel measured and instrument-like rather than playful — this is a coaching tool, not a game. Fourth, the Known Limits screen is a first-class destination with its own icon and its own presence in navigation, not a buried About-page footnote — that's not just an aesthetic choice, it directly serves the project's top priority of honest capability reporting. Fifth, demo sessions get a persistent, glanceable visual treatment in History (a distinct dashed border and a small permanent badge on the row and in the detail view), not a subtle text label that's one careless scroll away from being mistaken for a real session.

Now the flows and states, because this app has real complexity to get right. Home/Capture has to support four states and I'd add a fifth transient one: empty/first-run, capability-blocked (with three distinct honest sub-states — no camera hardware, access denied with a Settings deep-link, access restricted — each with its own icon and remedy, never collapsed into one gray "unavailable" card), ready-to-record (this is where camera placement guidance lives — a translucent silhouette the user's body must align with before the Start action even enables, so the app teaches correct framing physically rather than via a tooltip nobody reads), in-session (live tally + timer + a small "Detected: Squat" classifier chip), and a brief finalizing state right after End is tapped while the session is written and reconciled — this shouldn't be invisible or feel frozen, even if it's under a second.

One thing I want to push on that I don't think has been resolved yet: if the classifier misdetects the exercise, there's currently no way to correct it, and a wrong auto-detect means the wrong geometric rule silently runs for the whole set — that's arguably worse than the honest-limitations problem this whole project cares about, because it's the app confidently doing the wrong thing rather than admitting it doesn't know. I want the classifier chip to be tappable to override to one of the three enabled exercises, cheap to build, and it directly protects the credibility of the core loop.

After a session ends I want a lightweight Post-Set Summary sheet before landing in History — final count or hold duration, the one specific flag if it fired and how many reps it hit, and a single "Done" action. This is a small addition beyond what's been locked so far, and I recognize the risk flagged earlier about screen-surface competing with algorithm work, so I'd keep it to a sheet reusing Session Detail's content rather than a new full screen — the payoff is real: ending a plank hold and being dumped straight into a list is an anticlimactic, unsatisfying way to close the one moment the whole app exists for.

History needs calendar and list views, with demo sessions visually fenced as above; Session Detail shows reps/duration or hold-metric, the flag log, and a clip thumbnail only where one exists, with an honest "open on Mac to step through frames" affordance rather than a dead button on iPhone. Charts need their own empty state when there's under two sessions ("log a couple more sets to see a trend" with the exact action to do that), and must visibly disclose when demo sessions are excluded from the aggregate rather than silently having a shorter axis. Settings should never show a live-looking control for a feature not actually built this cycle — a dead export/erase button is worse than not having the row at all, so anything should-have that isn't finished should route straight into an explanation on the Known Limits screen. Known Limits itself needs a real empty state too, worded honestly ("no capability gaps detected in this Simulator/device run") rather than a bare "nothing here."

The point I'd fight hardest for on accessibility: two of the three exercises (push-up, plank) put the user's face toward the floor, meaning they cannot see the screen while it matters most. Haptic-per-rep is already required, but I think that's not enough by itself — the form-flag moment needs an optional distinct audio cue too, because a purely visual amber annotation is invisible to someone in push-up position. Dynamic Type has to scale the hero rep counter without breaking its container. Capability-blocked states need icon-plus-text, not color-only, distinctions. And VoiceOver needs an explicit spoken announcement on each counted rep and on a fired flag, since this product's core use case is literally "using the app without looking at it."
