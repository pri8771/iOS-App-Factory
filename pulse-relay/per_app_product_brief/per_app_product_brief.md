Pulse Relay should be built for one specific audience: parents or guardians of children under 13 who belong to a known school-pickup pod and occasionally lose pickup continuity at the last minute. The app exists because that moment is high-stakes and emotionally expensive: group chats are too ambiguous, and emergency services / taxi apps are wrong products for a trust-graph-only handoff. A real-time relay with explicit server-side claim arbitration and a single source of truth prevents duplicate coverage and removes uncertainty, which is a materially different outcome from messaging.

The core loop is: compose and post a same-day request with one tap and optional short note; pod members receive push that deep-links directly to the live request card; first person to accept gets a locked claim via server arbitration; everyone else sees “already covered by X” in seconds; if the accepter must drop out, they release and the request reopens instantly with re-notify; if no one accepts, the card escalates with visible elapsed-time “still open” status and explicit re-broadcast; once handoff is completed, the app writes one trust-relevant artifact that is both internal audit evidence and a shareable privacy-filtered receipt without child PII. States must be visible and explicit at every step, and nothing can show “accepted/completed/synced” until backend-acknowledged.

Paid value is the reduction of real uncertainty: parents pay for a faster, safer pickup response and for accountability that group chat can never provide. A viable model is family + pod capacity based on team needs (for example, larger pods with more than one child group per day), durable receipt retention and export windows, advanced escalation policies (auto-repeat windows and fallback channels after X minutes), and response-time analytics for parents and pods that want to improve coordination. In v1, this can be documented as phased product direction while shipping the free utility loop first, because the current constraints require no in-app payments during first build.

The competitive wedge is being a deterministic relay infrastructure instead of yet another parent thread: no maps-first experience, no stranger matching, no payments, no recurring task engine, no chat bloat. The product’s trust claim is narrow and honest: “you coordinate with known pod members,” never “verified” in ways the system does not provide.

Viral or niche growth is inherent in how the value is consumed, not bolted on: a pod is the launch mechanism. ## Final Output
