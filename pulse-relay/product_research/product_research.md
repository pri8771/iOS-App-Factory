## Final Output

**Concept framing (carried forward, sharpened):** this is a micro-supply network for immediate care continuity — coordinating a temporary, trusted handoff among people who already know each other, not a marketplace or social-discovery product. Audience: parents/guardians of children under 13 in a specific, pre-existing trust circle (one classroom, one daycare room, or one carpool cluster), hit by urgent same-day pickup/handoff disruptions. The real incumbent this app displaces is a class WhatsApp/GroupMe thread, whose gap is exactly what makes a backend load-bearing: no live acceptance state, no de-duplication of multiple "I got it" replies, no accountability record.

**Comparable patterns (all ASSUMPTION, not verified via external research):** Life360 (location/family-safety — avoid becoming a map-centric knockoff), ClassDojo/Brightwheel (school-parent messaging, not live matching), Nextdoor (avoid the "open post board" feel — this must read as pod-scoped and personal), HopSkipDrive (paid background-checked driver model, explicitly rejected as the trust model per the prior phase), Uber/Lyft-style matching (proven logistics UX, wrong trust model for child handoff).

**Scope for the first build, now locked:**
- One pod type only: school pickup. No daycare or activity variants in v1.
- No GPS/live location anywhere in v1 — explicitly deferred to a possible v2, not quietly smuggled in as "ETA-aware" logic.
- No child PII: no child names, photos, or face data stored. Requests reference "your child" generically.
- Join flow: invite-link or admin/pod-issued join code, with a real "you're not in a pod yet" empty state and a clear CTA to join or start a class pod — not a dead end that requires pre-existing pod membership to do anything.
- The accept flow's race condition is a first-build acceptance criterion: server-side first-accept-wins via a real conditional/compare-and-swap update, the losing acceptor gets an explicit "already claimed" state (never a silent or ambiguous outcome), target sub-30-second propagation to both devices in the happy path, and a named test case — two authenticated devices racing an accept on the same request — that must be actually run, not just code-reviewed.
- One shared data object, two views: an internal audit-log/evidence entry (requester, accepter, timestamps, consent) and a derived, privacy-filtered "relay receipt" card (e.g. "Covered by a member of [Pod Name] in 6 minutes") with no child name or address — this card is the deliberate, concrete viral/sharing mechanic, not a bolted-on invite button.
- All copy uses "self-vouched / pod-vetted" language and never implies identity verification or background-check vetting the app doesn't perform.
- Cold-start/network-density is explicitly out of scope to "prove" in this build cycle; the mitigation is minimizing marginal join friction (one link, one tap, Sign in with Apple) rather than attempting to simulate a real 15–20 family pod. A second real seeded pod-member account is a required, first-class part of this app's own verification protocol, since a single-account walkthrough cannot exercise the core feature.
- Exactly two funnels for v1: (1) post a relay request into your pod, (2) accept → resolve conflict → complete a handoff. Every screen in both funnels must show real empty/loading/error/success states, reachable from a push notification action through to assigned/claimed/conflict/completed, including an offline fallback.
