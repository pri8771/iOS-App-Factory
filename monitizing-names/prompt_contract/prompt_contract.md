Original prompt, verbatim: "We want to make a website to sell the naming rights of my future child. and potentially, make a platform where anyone can sell the naming rights of their future children. It should be a web app with great design."

Before anyone gets excited about the concept, I want to flag the thing that will bite us if we don't name it now: "naming rights" to a child are not a real transferable legal asset in basically any jurisdiction. Parents choose the name on the birth certificate; nobody can contractually bind a future child's legal name to a stranger's payment. So this has to be built and worded as a novelty/publicity/crowdfunding experience — closer to "pay to influence a name, winner gets credit and a personal promise" — not as an actual legal rights marketplace. If the copy anywhere implies "you are legally purchasing the right to name this child," that's a false claim the interface would be making about an outcome that cannot actually happen, which is exactly the kind of thing the global rules say we must never do. My assumption going in: this is a fun/sincere personal site with a real offer-and-selection mechanic (money or pledges, a shortlist, a winner), explicitly disclaimed as non-binding and at the parents' sole discretion. If that assumption is wrong, the whole product framing changes, so it needs to be stated, not smuggled in.

Second risk: the prompt has two asks bundled with different confidence levels — "sell naming rights of MY future child" (stated as the actual ask) and "potentially, make a platform where anyone can sell..." (hedged with "potentially"). I don't think we should treat those as equally hard requirements. My read: the personal single-listing site is the MVP and must be fully real and working; the multi-user marketplace is an explicit stretch goal that should be attempted but is allowed to be the thing that gets cut if we're at risk of shipping a half-built mess, per "prefer a smaller finished app over a larger half-built app." What must not happen is silently dropping the multi-user idea without saying so, and it also must not happen that we build a shallow single-listing page and call the marketplace ambition "done" by implication.

Third risk, and the one I'd bet actual money breaks first: this is user-generated content about children, submitted by anonymous visitors, with a bidding/payment surface. That's a magnet for abuse — offensive name proposals, spam listings, people impersonating other people's pregnancies, minors registering. Even in an MVP, I want a reporting/flagging affordance on every listing and every submitted offer, and a basic content boundary in the submission form (length limits, no spam links), otherwise the first thing anyone screenshots and shares is the ugliest offer on the leaderboard. Also: no real personal data about an actual minor should be required anywhere (no requiring a real name, photo, or birthdate of a real child) — the listing should work with placeholder-safe fields like due month, a nickname/alias, and a short story.

Fourth: "sell" implies money. We should not wire up a live payment processor with real card charges inside a generated app run — that's a compliance and safety surface (real charges, refunds, chargebacks, escrow-style disputes over who "won") far beyond what this build can respectably own. My decision rule: build the complete offer/bid/checkout UX end to end, but the actual settlement is simulated and clearly labeled as such (e.g., "Demo Checkout" / "No real charge will occur"), unless the user explicitly asks for and confirms a real Stripe-type integration later. That satisfies "sell" as a genuine interactive product experience without making a promise ("Payment received," "Funds transferred") that isn't true.

Fifth, "great design" is unfalsifiable as written, so it needs to cash out into things we can actually check: full light/dark mode, no overlapping/clipped text on the offer leaderboard (this is exactly the kind of screen where dollar amounts and names will collide if not de-cluttered), real empty/loading/success/error states on every flow (empty listing with zero offers must be designed content, not a blank div), and no default unstyled browser form chrome sitting next to custom-styled components.
